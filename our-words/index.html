Our Nine Words

Nine key words that make our school unmistakably ours—and help us aim the decisions that matter.

Who we are should shape what we do. These nine words are not aspirations invented for a page—they are the vocabulary of who we are, drawn from the commitments already written into our prospectus, our website, and our founding documents. Three words describe who our graduates become. Three name what makes us unlike any other school in our community. And three are non-negotiable: true today, and true no matter how much we grow.

Graduates

who our graduates become

True steadfast in character and aim

This word describes us more than any other. Our mantra is Launched true, for God and community. Our motto is Sagitta vera—the true arrow. Our purpose is to launch graduates strong in character, true in aim. To be true is to be steadfast: unswerving, stable, straight—a shaft free from the bends of compromise, holding its course whatever the winds. Discipline is the means; true is the result.

Disciplined habits forged at the bench and the range

Discipline runs through everything we do. The arrow’s shaft is forged in discipline and truth. Archery and woodworking teach the quiet focus required to shape both tools and character. The Archer draws the bow with discipline and a servant’s heart. Our graduates leave with the habits—self-discipline, respect, perseverance—that make servant leadership possible.

Discerning thinkers who test everything against truth

The Trivium forges ordered, articulate, and discerning minds. Our graduates do not merely absorb information; they test everything against truth. They think critically, communicate persuasively, and love learning—thinkers who can tell what is good and true from what is merely loud.

Uniques

what no other school in our community is

Whole-Person mind, body, and soul formed together

Our mission is to forge students as whole persons—mind, body, and soul. Mind at the Great Books. Body at the woodworking bench and the archery range. Soul in prayer, Scripture, and worship. No other school in our community forms all three together, and we will never trade formation for mere efficiency.

Classical the Trivium, Great Books, a higher calling

Our academic approach is the time-tested classical tradition: the Trivium of Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric, matched to each stage of childhood, alongside Latin, the Great Books, and a senior thesis written and defended publicly. Rigor alone is not our distinctive—many schools claim rigor. The classical tradition is, because it calls every student to something higher: to think clearly, work skillfully, and grow in virtue.

Known small by design; every student known

We are small by design. Every student is known. Every family is partnered with. Every graduate is launched with care. It is the first thing a visitor feels walking through our doors: here, the teachers truly care, and no child is anonymous.

Non-Negotiables

what never changes

Christ sacred, central, unapologetic

Christ is sacred here—not one subject among many, but the center around which our entire curriculum and culture are ordered. We are unapologetically Christian: every lesson seeks what is true and good, every relationship reflects His love, and every day begins with prayer and Scripture.

Stretched-and-Loved high standards held with kindness, daily

Every student, every day, experiences both: high standards held with kindness—courage with compassion. Because every student is an image-bearer of God, capable of great things, we will neither coddle nor crush. Either half alone would make us a different, and lesser, school.

Faithful to God’s truth and to Englewood, at any size

However much we grow, we remain faithful—to God’s truth in Scripture and to the neighborhood we were founded to serve. Growth will never mean drift: not from the Word, and not from Englewood.

How We Use These Words

When a significant decision is before us—a hire, a curriculum choice, a facility, a partnership—these words help us ask: Is this a yes to who we are? A choice that would make our graduates less true, our formation less whole, or our foundation less Christ-centered is a no—no matter how good the opportunity.

Sagitta vera—launched true, for God and community.
