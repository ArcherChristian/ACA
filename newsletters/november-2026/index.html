Englewood’s Mornings

A full room on a Thursday morning in Carol Stream, and what comes next.

 

Thursday, October 29, Christ Presbyterian Church, Carol Stream.

Dear Friends,

At three minutes past seven last Thursday morning, October 29, Daniel looked out at about 100 faces, and we got to celebrate and share God’s work in Englewood as the Lord leads us closer to opening the doors of Archer Christian Academy. For those of you who were there, thank you! We know many more of you were unable to be, and we wanted to take the opportunity to celebrate and share a little of that event. Here is the short version…

Russ Gregg, who co-founded Hope Academy in Minneapolis twenty-five years ago, shared about Hope’s journey in providing high-quality education to those who are academically disenfranchised, culturally diverse, and economically disadvantaged. He reminded us that students who grew up in overlooked communities are just as equally made in the image of God, and offered us all the opportunity to see them through his eyes, inviting us to remember that we carry the burden of caring for our neighbor’s needs, and ushering them to glory.

Russ Gregg reminding us of the importance of seeing others as image bearers (and not only feeding people hot dogs).

Afterward, Daniel was able to share briefly (to the surprise of those who have heard him preach!) about Englewood, describing a typical Thursday afternoon at the ministry when the doorbell rings, and rings and rings, as twenty or so kids come through the gate. Elementary and middle school kids who have found a safe place and people who show the love and grace of Christ to them day after day. He shared about the ceiling our programs are hitting as we fight for the scraps of the day, working with distracted and tired kids who are being failed by a broken system.

We were also blessed to be able to share Ray’s story. Ray first came through our door in fifth grade; he is in his twenties now and runs the after-school club with Dana. We asked him what a school like Archer would have meant to him. “I feel like I was Jonah,” he said, “and God sent the big fish after me. This ministry was the big fish… [and] it would be an even bigger fish if the school was involved.” We invite you to watch the five minute video here and be encouraged to see God’s work in his life.

We also want to invite you to join in on what God is doing in Englewood. It will take $400,000 to open the doors to forty-two kindergartners, first graders, and second graders on August 23, 2027. In total, at this moment, roughly $250,000 of the $400,000 has been committed. That leaves about $150,000 to raise, which will cover teachers and other staff; finding and preparing a building; furniture, books, and curriculum; and everything else a school needs before the first bell rings. We invite you to participate in giving and in prayer.

We are convinced we cannot do this work without prayer. Most notably, we still do not have a school home. We are looking for our first one among the churches of Englewood, and we would ask you to keep praying that the right door opens, and soon. This becomes increasingly important as we start talking to interested families, many of whom don’t own a car, and will need to know how their children will get to school each day. Pray also for the right people to lead and to teach, and for the forty-two families we will welcome on Day 1.

Would you pray with us?

For a school home among the churches of Englewood, and soon. For the right people to lead and to teach. And for the forty-two families we have not yet met.
