Why Englewood

The Need, in Numbers

A neighborhood with too few options and too much at stake—the case for Archer, as the numbers tell it.

The Academic Need

Most Students Are Not Yet Proficient

On the 2025 Illinois Assessment of Readiness, fewer than half of Chicago Public Schools students in grades 3–8 met the state standard in English language arts, and about one in four met it in mathematics.

40.6% Proficient in English Language Arts Chicago Public Schools, grades 3–8, 2025
26.2% Proficient in Mathematics Chicago Public Schools, grades 3–8, 2025

Source: Chicago Public Schools, 2025 Illinois Assessment of Readiness results, as reported on the 2025 Illinois Report Card. Illinois set new proficiency benchmarks in 2025; results from earlier years are not comparable.

The Community

The Families We Serve

Archer will open at 672 West Marquette Road, near 67th and Union, in the neighborhood where Urban Youth Outreach has served families for more than forty years. The economics of the neighborhood shape the model: tuition set by what each family is able to give, with partners covering the rest.

$31,397 Median Household Income Englewood, 2020–2024
44.9% Households Earning Under $25,000 Englewood, 2020–2024
23.0% Unemployment Rate Englewood, 2020–2024
12.0% Adults With a Bachelor’s Degree or Higher Englewood, 2020–2024

Source: Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning, Englewood Community Data Snapshot (June 2026 release), from the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 American Community Survey five-year estimates.

Putting It Together

What the Numbers Say

1

A Clear Academic Need

Across the city, most children are not yet reading or doing math at grade level. Englewood needs a school built for rigor from the first day of kindergarten, so that no child has to be rescued later from a trajectory set early.

2

An Educational Desert

There is no classical Christian school in Englewood, and only a handful across the South Side. Archer would not be competing with anyone. It would be filling a void.

3

A School Families Can Walk To

A school at 67th and Union spares children the long bus rides and transfers that keep many from reaching a good school across the city—and it can anchor the block for decades.
